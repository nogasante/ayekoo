THE SIX AGRO-ECOLOGICAL ZONES OF GHANA

Ghana is divided into six agro-ecological zones on the basis of climate. From
north to south they are: Sudan Savannah Zone, Guinea Savannah Zone, Transition
Zone, Semi-deciduous Forest Zone, Rain Forest Zone, and Coastal Savannah Zone.

RAINFALL PATTERN: ONE SEASON OR TWO

The rainfall pattern is uni-modal in the Sudan Savannah and Guinea Savannah
zones, meaning one rainy season a year. It is bi-modal in all the other zones,
meaning a major and a minor rainy season.

This is why northern Ghana has a single cropping season while the south has two.

RAINY SEASONS BY ZONE

Rain Forest zone: major rainy season March to July, minor rainy season September
to November. Mean annual rainfall 2 200 mm, range 800-2 800 mm. Area 9 500 km2.

Deciduous Forest zone: major rainy season March to July, minor rainy season
September to November. Mean annual rainfall 1 500 mm, range 1 200-1 600 mm.
Area 66 000 km2.

Transitional zone: major rainy season March to July, minor rainy season
September to October. Mean annual rainfall 1 300 mm, range 1 100-1 400 mm.
Area 8 400 km2.

Coastal Savannah zone: major rainy season March to July, minor rainy season
September to October. Mean annual rainfall 800 mm, range 600-1 200 mm.
Area 4 500 km2.

Guinea Savannah zone: one rainy season only, May to September. Mean annual
rainfall 1 000 mm, range 800-1 200 mm. Area 147 900 km2.

Sudan Savannah zone: one rainy season only, May to September. Mean annual
rainfall 1 000 mm. Area 2 200 km2.

RAINFALL ACROSS THE COUNTRY

Mean annual rainfall ranges from 800 mm in the Coastal Savannah to 2 200 mm in
the Rain Forest. The Tropical Eastern Coastal Belt is warm and comparatively
dry; the southwest is hot and humid; the north is relatively hot and dry
compared with other parts of the country.

WHICH REGIONS FALL IN WHICH ZONE

The only region-to-zone statement corroborated by a source in this corpus is
that the Sudan Savannah Zone includes districts in the Upper East Region.

No document in this corpus gives a full mapping of Ghana's administrative
regions to agro-ecological zones. That mapping is therefore not recorded here,
and a question naming a town or region rather than a zone cannot yet be resolved
to a zone automatically. This is a known gap to be filled from a citable source.
