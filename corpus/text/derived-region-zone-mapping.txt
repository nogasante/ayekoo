WHICH AGRO-ECOLOGICAL ZONE A REGION FALLS IN

Source: Ghana Meteorological Agency, Weather and Climate Manual for Agriculture
(2022). Ghana is divided into six ecological zones: Sudan Savannah, Guinea
Savannah, Transition Zone, Deciduous Forest, Evergreen (moist and wet evergreen)
or Rainforest, and Coastal Savannah.

IMPORTANT — READ THIS BEFORE USING THE LIST

Zones do not follow regional borders. Several regions lie across two or more
zones, so a region name narrows the answer without always settling it.

GMet writes with the ten regions that existed before 2018. Ghana now has
sixteen. In particular, the former Brong Ahafo region was split into Bono, Bono
East and Ahafo, and Western North, Oti, Savannah and North East were created.
Where this document says "Brong Ahafo", it covers what are now Bono, Bono East
and Ahafo.

GUINEA SAVANNAH ZONE

Covers the whole of Upper West and Northern regions. Also occupies parts of
Upper East region, and the northern part of Brong Ahafo and Volta region.
One rainfall season, lasting from May to October. Annual rainfall about 1000mm.

SUDAN SAVANNAH ZONE

Spreads from the north-eastern part of Upper East region.
Annual rainfall between 500mm and 700mm.

TRANSITION ZONE

Falls within the middle portion of Brong Ahafo region, the northern part of both
Ashanti and Eastern regions, and the western part of the Volta region.
Two rainy seasons. Annual rainfall about 1200mm.

DECIDUOUS FOREST ZONE

Cuts across the northern part of Western region, through southern Brong Ahafo,
Ashanti and Eastern regions. Also spreads to the Volta region and most part of
the Central region.
Two rainy seasons. Annual rainfall about 1400mm.

EVERGREEN OR RAINFOREST ZONE

Most parts of the Western region are within the Evergreen zone. It also captures
a small part of Central region.
Records the most annual rainfall, about 2000mm. Same rainfall pattern as the
Transition Zone and Deciduous Forest, that is two rainy seasons.

COASTAL SAVANNAH ZONE

Stretches from Central region through Greater Accra to Volta regions.
GMet records one rainy season and annual rainfall of about 600mm.

WHERE SOURCES DISAGREE

GMet and FAO do not agree about the Coastal Savannah. GMet states it has a
single rainy season with about 600mm of rain a year. FAO's *Fertilizer Use by
Crop in Ghana* records the Coastal Savannah as bi-modal — a major season from
March to July and a minor season from September to October — with mean annual
rainfall of 800mm and a range of 600 to 1200mm.

Both figures are reproduced here rather than reconciled, because reconciling
them would mean choosing between two national institutions on no authority. A
farmer in the coastal strip planning a minor-season crop should treat the
question as open and ask an extension officer.
